## 🧠 Super Study Tools

### [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/)
**What it is:** Google's AI-powered research assistant.
**How to use it:** Upload your PDFs, notes, or articles. It creates summaries, flashcards, and even podcasts explaining your content.
**Perfect for:** Turning boring textbooks into audio briefings or quiz yourself before an exam.

### [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/)
**What it is:** The gold standard for spaced repetition flashcards.
**How to use it:** Download pre-made decks for coding, networking, or certifications. Or make your own.
**Perfect for:** Memorizing ports, commands, syntax, and definitions forever.

### [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/)
**What it is:** A powerful note-taking app that links your thoughts together.
**How to use it:** Build your own "second brain" of IT knowledge.
**Perfect for:** Connecting concepts (e.g., linking "HTTP" to "Web Development" to "Security").

## 🏆 Legendary Free Courses ("The Hall of Fame")

These are not just tutorials; they are **journeys**. They are hard, they are respected, and they look amazing on a resume.

### 1. [Harvard CS50x: Introduction to Computer Science](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/)
- **Level:** Absolute Beginner to Hero.
- **What you get:** A real Harvard certificate (free if you audit, paid if you verify).
- **Why it's legendary:** It changes the way you think. You won't just learn code; you'll learn how computers *think*.
- **Commitment:** 10-20 hours/week for 12 weeks.
- **Warning:** It is challenging. Do not give up at Problem Set 1.

### 2. [Nand2Tetris (Build a Computer from First Principles)](https://www.nand2tetris.org/)
- **Level:** Intermediate (Do this after some basics).
- **What you get:** You literally build a computer from logic gates (NAND) up to running a Tetris game.
- **Why it's legendary:** You will understand *everything* about how hardware and software talk to each other. No more magic black boxes.
- **Parts:** Part I (Hardware) and Part II (Software). Both are free on their site.

### 3. [MIT OpenCourseWare - Missing Semester](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/)
- **Level:** Beginner/Intermediate.
- **What you get:** The stuff universities forget to teach. Terminal mastery, Git, Vim, debugging, data wrangling.
- **Why it's legendary:** It fills the gap between "knowing code" and "being a power user."

## 📜 Free Certification Guides & Legal Exam Prep

Want to pass a cert without spending $500 on a bootcamp? Use these official and legal resources.

### 🔐 Security+ (CompTIA)
- **Official Practice Questions:** [CompTIA SecurityX Practice Tests](https://www.comptia.org/store/exams/security-x) (Paid but official)
- **Free/Legal Alternatives:**
    - [Professor Messer's Course Notes](https://www.professormesser.com/security-plus/sy0-601/sy0-601-video/sy0-601-comptia-security-plus-course-notes/) (Free videos, highly respected).
    - [ExamCompass](https://www.examcompass.com/) (Free practice quizzes, unofficial but accurate to the style).
    - **Tip:** Search for "CompTIA Security+ Acronyms" and memorize them. 50% of the exam is knowing what the acronym stands for.

### ☁️ AWS Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C01/C02)
- **Official Free Training:** [AWS Skill Builder](https://skillbuilder.aws/) (Official free digital training by Amazon).
- **Whitepapers:** Read the ["AWS Well-Architected Framework"](https://aws.amazon.com/architecture/well-architected/) (Free PDF from AWS).
- **Practice:** Create a free AWS account and follow the "First Run" wizards.

### 🐧 Linux Essentials (LPI)
- **Official Exam Objectives:** [LPI Linux Essentials Objectives](https://www.lpi.org/our-certifications/linux-essentials-objectives/) (Download the PDF, it tells you EXACTLY what is on the test).
- **Practice:** Install Ubuntu in a Virtual Machine and try to do everything without a mouse.

> **⚠️ A Note on "Brain Dumps":**
> You might find websites selling "real exam questions." **DO NOT USE THEM.**
> 1. It is often illegal/copyright infringement.
> 2. If caught, you can be banned from certification programs for life.
> 3. You won't actually learn anything.
> Stick to official practice tests and open-source study guides!
