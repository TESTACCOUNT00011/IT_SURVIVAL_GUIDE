# 06 - Passwords & Security: Don't Get Hacked

## 🎯 The Harsh Truth

**Most people get hacked because of lazy security habits.**

Not because hackers are geniuses. Because people:
- Use `password123` as their password
- Use the same password everywhere
- Click on suspicious links
- Never update their software

**You can avoid 99% of problems by following this guide.**

---

## 🔐 Password Basics

### ❌ Bad Passwords (Never Use These)

```
password
password123
123456789
qwerty
iloveyou
your name or birthdate
your pet's name
"Welcome1" (corporate default)
```

Hackers have lists of these. They try them FIRST.

### ✅ Good Password Characteristics

A strong password has:
- **Length:** At least 12-16 characters (longer = better)
- **Variety:** Upper + lowercase + numbers + symbols
- **Uniqueness:** NOT used anywhere else
- **Randomness:** No dictionary words or personal info

### Examples of Good Passwords

```
Correct Horse Battery Staple (passphrase)
Tr0ub4dor&3 (but don't use this exact one!)
BlueCoffee$Mug#RainyDay2024
```

**Best approach:** Use a passphrase (multiple random words) with some symbols/numbers.

---

## 🎰 The Golden Rule: NEVER Reuse Passwords

**Why?** If ONE site gets hacked, hackers try that password EVERYWHERE.

Example:
1. You use `MyPassword123!` for Facebook and your bank
2. Facebook gets hacked (not even your fault!)
3. Hackers now have your email + password
4. They try it on your bank... and get in 😱

**Every account needs a UNIQUE password.**

## 🏆 Password Managers (Life-Changing Tool)

A password manager:
- Generates strong, random passwords for every account
- Stores them securely (encrypted)
- Auto-fills them when you log in
- You only need to remember ONE password (the master password)

### ✅ Recommended: Bitwarden

**Why Bitwarden?**
- ✅ Free (really free, not "free trial")
- ✅ Open source (anyone can verify it's secure)
- ✅ Works on all devices
- ✅ Easy to use
- ✅ Trusted by security experts

**Other good options:**
- 1Password (paid, but excellent)
- KeePass (free, but more technical)

**❌ Avoid:**
- Browser's built-in password saver (not secure enough)
- Writing passwords in a document (huge risk)
- Sticky notes on your monitor (just... no)

## 🛡️ Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

### What Is 2FA?

2FA = Two steps to log in:
1. Something you KNOW (password)
2. Something you HAVE (your phone, a code generator)

Even if someone steals your password, they can't get in without your phone.

### Types of 2FA

| Type | How It Works | Security Level |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **SMS codes** | Text message with code | ⭐⭐ Okay, but not great |
| **Authenticator app** | App generates codes | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Much better |
| **Hardware key** | Physical USB device | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Best (but expensive) |

### ✅ Recommended: Authenticator Apps

**Free options:**
- **Google Authenticator** (simple, reliable)
- **Authy** (backups, multi-device)
- **Raivo OTP** (iOS, open source)
- **Aegis** (Android, open source)

### Where to Enable 2FA (Priority Order)

Enable 2FA on these IMMEDIATELY:
1. 🚨 Email account (Gmail, Outlook, etc.)
2. 🚨 Bank/financial accounts
3. 🚨 Social media (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter)
4. 🚨 Cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox)
5. Any account with payment info

**How to enable:**
1. Go to account Settings → Security
2. Look for "Two-Factor Authentication" or "2FA"
3. Follow the steps (usually scan a QR code with your app)
4. **SAVE THE BACKUP CODES** (print them or store safely)

## 🎣 Phishing: Don't Take the Bait

### What Is Phishing?

Phishing = Fake emails/messages designed to trick you into:
- Giving your password
- Downloading malware
- Sending money
- Sharing personal info

### 🚩 Red Flags of Phishing

| Red Flag | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Urgent/threatening language | "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!" |
| Too good to be true | "You won $1,000,000! Click here!" |
| Strange sender address | `support@amaz0n-security.com` (not Amazon!) |
| Generic greeting | "Dear Customer" instead of your name |
| Suspicious links | Hover over link—does it match the claimed website? |
| Attachments you didn't expect | Random invoice.zip from unknown sender |
| Asking for passwords | NO legitimate company asks for passwords via email |

### ✅ How to Protect Yourself

1. **Don't click links in emails** — go directly to the website
2. **Check the sender's email address** carefully
3. **Hover over links** before clicking (see where they really go)
4. **Never download attachments** from unknown senders
5. **When in doubt, contact the company directly** (use official website, not the email)

### Example: Real vs Fake

❌ **Fake Email:**
```
From: security@paypa1-alerts.com
Subject: URGENT: Your Account Suspended!

Dear Customer,

Your PayPal account has been suspended. Click here immediately 
to verify your information or your account will be permanently closed.

[VERIFY NOW]
```

✅ **Real PayPal Communication:**
- Comes from official @paypal.com domain
- Addresses you by name
- Never asks you to click urgent links
- You can log in directly at paypal.com to check status

## 🔄 Software Updates: Boring But Critical

### Why Updates Matter

Updates fix:
- Security holes hackers can exploit
- Bugs that crash your software
- Performance issues

**Running outdated software = leaving your door unlocked.**

### What to Update

| Software | How Often | How to Update |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Windows/macOS** | When prompted | Settings → Update |
| **Browser** | Automatic (usually) | Check in Help menu |
| **Phone (iOS/Android)** | When prompted | Settings → System Update |
| **Apps** | Regularly | App Store / Play Store |
| **Antivirus** | Daily (automatic) | Should be automatic |

### Enable Automatic Updates

Wherever possible, turn on automatic updates:
- Windows: Settings → Update & Security → Advanced → Automatic
- Mac: System Preferences → Software Update → Automatically keep up to date
- Phone: Settings → Software Update → Auto-download
- Browser: Usually automatic by default

## 🦠 Antivirus: Do You Need It?

### Windows 10/11

**Windows Defender** (built-in) is actually good now!
- ✅ Free
- ✅ Always on
- ✅ Doesn't slow your computer
- ✅ Catches most threats

**You don't need Norton, McAfee, etc.** They're mostly unnecessary nowadays.

### Mac

Macs are less targeted but NOT immune.
- Be careful what you download
- Keep macOS updated
- Consider Malwarebytes (free version) for occasional scans

### Android

- Only install apps from Google Play Store
- Keep phone updated
- Built-in Google Play Protect is usually enough

### iPhone

- iOS is very secure by design
- Only install from App Store
- Keep iOS updated
- You're probably fine without antivirus

## 📱 Mobile Security

### Basic Rules

1. **Use a passcode/biometric lock** (NOT just swipe to unlock)
2. **Update your phone** when prompted
3. **Only install from official app stores** (App Store, Google Play)
4. **Review app permissions** (why does flashlight need your contacts?)
5. **Don't click links in random texts** ("smishing" is real)

### Find My Phone

Enable this NOW:
- **iPhone:** Settings → [Your Name] → Find My → Find My iPhone
- **Android:** Settings → Security → Find My Device

If you lose your phone, you can:
- Locate it on a map
- Lock it remotely
- Wipe data remotely

## ☁️ Backup Your Data

### The 3-2-1 Rule

- **3** copies of your data
- **2** different types of storage
- **1** copy offsite (cloud or different location)

### Simple Backup Strategy

**For most people:**
1. **Cloud backup:** Google Drive, OneDrive, or iCloud (automatic)
2. **External hard drive:** Plug in once a week to backup important files

**What to backup:**
- Documents
- Photos
- Anything you'd cry about losing

**What you DON'T need to backup:**
- Programs/apps (can reinstall)
- Temporary files
- Downloads folder (mostly junk)
