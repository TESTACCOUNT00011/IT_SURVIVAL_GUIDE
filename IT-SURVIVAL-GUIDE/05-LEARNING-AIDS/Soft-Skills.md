# Soft Skills & Professionalism Module

Technical skills get you interviews. **Soft skills get you the job and promotions.** This module covers the unwritten rules of tech professionalism.

---

## 1. How to Ask Good Questions

Asking questions is a skill. Bad questions waste everyone's time. Good questions show you're thoughtful.

### The "Before You Ask" Checklist

- [ ] Have I Googled this first? (Use [[../02-BASICS/04-Google-Fu|Google-Fu techniques]])
- [ ] Did I read the error message carefully?
- [ ] Have I checked the documentation?
- [ ] Did I search the community forum for existing answers?
- [ ] Can I reproduce the issue consistently?

### How to Frame Your Question

**❌ Bad Example:**
> "My code doesn't work. Help!"

**✅ Good Example:**
> "I'm trying to fetch data from an API in React, but I'm getting a CORS error. 
> 
> **What I've tried:**
> - Added headers to my fetch request
> - Checked that the API endpoint is correct
> - Tested in Postman (works there)
> 
> **Error message:** 
> `Access to fetch at 'https://api.example.com' from origin 'http://localhost:3000' has been blocked by CORS policy`
> 
> **My code:**
> ```javascript
> [paste relevant snippet]
> ```
> 
> What am I missing?"

### Where to Ask

| Platform | Best For | Tips |
|----------|----------|------|
| Stack Overflow | Specific technical errors | Search first, be precise |
| Discord/Slack communities | Quick guidance | Check channel topics |
| Reddit (r/learnprogramming) | Career advice, learning paths | Read subreddit rules |
| GitHub Issues | Bugs in specific libraries | Follow issue template |
| Mentors/Colleagues | Career guidance, code review | Respect their time |

## 2. Reading Documentation

Documentation is your best friend. Learning to read it efficiently is crucial.

### Strategy

1. **Start with "Getting Started"** - Don't skip basics
2. **Scan the Table of Contents** - Understand the structure
3. **Read API/Reference sections** when implementing specific features
4. **Check examples** - Most docs have code samples
5. **Look for "Common Pitfalls"** or troubleshooting sections

### When Docs Are Unclear

- Check the GitHub repo's issues (others may have asked)
- Look for blog tutorials on the same topic
- Watch video walkthroughs
- Ask in communities with specific questions about the confusion

## 3. Handling Imposter Syndrome

**Fact:** 70%+ of tech professionals experience imposter syndrome. You're not alone.

### Signs You Have It

- "Everyone else knows more than me"
- "I got lucky, they'll figure out I'm a fraud"
- "I need to learn everything before applying"
- Feeling anxious about asking questions

### Reality Checks

✅ **Senior engineers Google things daily**  
✅ **No one knows everything**—tech is too vast  
✅ **You were hired because you CAN do the job**  
✅ **Feeling confused means you're learning, not failing**

### Coping Strategies

1. **Keep a "Win Log"** - Document daily small victories
   - Used [[../../00-TEMPLATES/Learning-Summary|Learning Summary]] to track progress
   - Celebrate finishing projects, not just understanding everything

2. **Compare Yourself to Past You** - Not to seniors with 10 years experience

3. **Talk About It** - You'll find others feel the same

4. **Remember:** Being comfortable being uncomfortable = growth

## 4. Email & Communication Etiquette

### Professional Email Structure

```
Subject: Clear and specific (e.g., "Question about Project X Timeline")

Hi [Name],

[Brief context - 1 sentence]

[Your question/request - be specific]

[What you've tried so far - if applicable]

[Specific ask - what do you need from them?]

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

### Slack/Discord Best Practices

- **Don't:** "Hey, can I ask you something?" (Just ask the question)
- **Do:** Post your full question with context immediately
- **Don't:** @channel for non-urgent issues
- **Do:** Use threads to keep channels clean
- **Do:** Wait reasonable time before following up (24-48 hours)

### Code Review Etiquette

**Giving Feedback:**
- Focus on code, not the person
- "This function could be clearer" vs "You wrote this badly"
- Suggest, don't command: "Consider extracting this into a helper function"

**Receiving Feedback:**
- Don't take it personally—it's about the code
- Ask clarifying questions if unsure
- Thank people for their time
- Implement or explain your reasoning (don't ignore)

## 5. Time Management & Avoiding Burnout

### The Pomodoro Technique

1. Work for 25 minutes (no distractions)
2. Take 5-minute break
3. After 4 cycles, take 15-30 minute break

**Tools:** pomofocus.io, Forest app, or simple timer

### Setting Boundaries

- **Define study hours** - Don't let tech consume all waking time
- **Take real breaks** - Walk away from screens
- **Sleep 7-8 hours** - Critical for memory consolidation
- **One rest day per week** - Minimum

### Signs of Burnout

- Dreading opening your laptop
- Irritability when coding
- Physical symptoms (headaches, eye strain)
- No longer enjoying projects you used to love

**If burning out:** Take 3-7 days completely off. You won't forget everything.

## 6. Networking Without Being Awkward

### Online Networking

**Good First Messages:**
> "Hi [Name], I saw your post about [topic] and found it really helpful. I'm currently learning [skill] and had a quick question about [specific thing]. Do you have 5 minutes to point me in the right direction?"

**Follow-Up After Getting Help:**
> "Thank you so much! That solved my issue. I really appreciate you taking the time to help."

### In-Person Meetups

**Before You Go:**
- Set a goal: "I'll talk to 3 new people"
- Prepare a 30-second intro about yourself
- Bring business cards or have LinkedIn QR ready

**Conversation Starters:**
- "What brings you to this meetup?"
- "What kind of work do you do?"
- "Working on anything interesting lately?"

**Exit Strategy:**
> "It was great meeting you! I'm going to grab some water/check out the next talk. Let's connect on LinkedIn!"

### Maintaining Relationships

- Comment on their posts occasionally
- Share articles they might find interesting
- Congratulate on new jobs/promotions
- Don't only reach out when you need something

## 7. Dealing with Difficult People

### The "Know-It-All" Who Condescends

**Response:** Stay professional, don't engage emotionally
> "Thanks for the feedback. I'll look into that approach."

Then quietly document their behavior if it becomes a pattern.

### The Ghost (People Who Don't Respond)

**After 48 hours:** Send polite follow-up
**After 1 week:** Try alternative contact or ask someone else
**Don't:** Send angry messages or guilt-trip

### The Toxic Community Member

**Actions:**
- Don't feed the trolls
- Report to moderators if violating community guidelines
- Find better communities (there are many!)

## 8. Growth Mindset vs Fixed Mindset

| Fixed Mindset | Growth Mindset |
|---------------|----------------|
| "I'm bad at this" | "I'm not good at this YET" |
| Avoids challenges | Embraces challenges |
| Gives up easily | Persists through obstacles |
| Ignores feedback | Learns from criticism |
| Feels threatened by others' success | Finds inspiration in others' success |

### Daily Affirmations

- "Mistakes are how I learn"
- "Every expert was once a beginner"
- "Progress over perfection"
- "I can figure this out with time and effort"

## 10. Knowing When to Push Through vs When to Quit

### Push Through When:
- You're frustrated but still learning
- The challenge is temporary (tight deadline, difficult bug)
- You believe in the end goal

### Quit (or Pivot) When:
- Consistently miserable for 2+ months
- Health is suffering (physical or mental)
- Values don't align with the work
- A different path excites you more

**Remember:** Quitting a path ≠ failure. It's strategic redirection.
